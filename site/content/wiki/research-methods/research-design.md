<section id="introduction" class="level1">

<p>Quantitative methods with hypothesis testing are designed around the principles of critical rationalism (Karl Popper). This philosophical position states that <em>theory can never be finally verified but only falsified.</em></p>
<p>Descriptive statistics can only describe the sample as a subset of the population. Inferential statistics draws conclusions about the population based on the sample.</p>
</section>

<section id="variance-as-basic-concept" class="level1">
<h2>Variance as basic concept</h2>
<ul>
<li>Primary variance:
<ul>
<li>Impact of (experimental) factors on the change / variantion of the output</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Secondary variance:
<ul>
<li>Variation of output caused by nuisance variables</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Error variance:
<ul>
<li>caused by measurement errors and random processes</li>
</ul></li>
</ul>

<h2>Quality criteria of experiments</h2>
<section id="internal-validity" class="level2">
<h3 class="anchored" data-anchor-id="internal-validity">Internal Validity</h3>
<ul>
<li>is <em>essential condition</em> for external validity.</li>
<li>Changes in dependent variable clearly attributable to changes in independent variables.</li>
<li>Increases with decreasing impact of nuisance variables (Quasi-Exp -&gt; Experiment).</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="external-validity" class="level2">
<h3 class="anchored" data-anchor-id="external-validity">External validity</h3>
<ul>
<li>Results of experiment can be generalized to population</li>
<li>increases with increasing naturalness (laboratory -&gt; field)</li>
<li>high internal validity can be problematic for external validity (labor vs.&nbsp;field)</li>
</ul>
<section id="population-validity" class="level4">
<h5 class="anchored" data-anchor-id="population-validity">Population validity</h5>
<ul>
<li>selection of representative sample</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="situation-validity" class="level4">
<h5 class="anchored" data-anchor-id="situation-validity">Situation validity</h5>
<ul>
<li>generalizable to situations deviating from experiment (ecological validity)</li>
</ul>
</section>
